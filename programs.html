Our Programs

Exposure. Access. Experience.

Meaningful Programs. Real-World Experiences. Opportunities to Beecome.

At Project Bridging, youth development is more than attending workshops or participating in activities. It is about creating consistent opportunities for young women to learn, explore, build relationships, develop skills, and experience the world beyond their everyday surroundings.

Our programs bring together culturally affirming mentorship, educational enrichment, leadership development, wellness, life skills, community engagement, and experiential learning.

Each program is designed to offer meaningful support during critical stages of growth and development while connecting young women to resources, relationships, and experiences that expand their opportunities.

From weekly mentorship and skill-building activities to educational excursions, leadership opportunities, and community projects, our young women are encouraged to participate, discover, question, create, serve, and lead.

Our Youth Development Pathways

Programs by Grade Band

Project Bridging serves young women through age-appropriate programs that recognize their changing developmental needs, interests, and experiences.

Junior Project Bridging — Grades K–4 | Building the Foundation

Junior Project Bridging introduces elementary-aged participants to positive relationships, character development, confidence-building, early leadership, creative learning, and community participation.

Through age-appropriate activities, young participants develop foundational social, emotional, communication, and life skills while exploring new interests and building friendships.

Areas of focus: Character, belonging, communication, creativity, early leadership, literacy, social skills, and exploration.

Project Bridging — Grades 5–8 | Discovering Identity and Building Confidence

Our middle school pathway provides guidance and support during a developmental period marked by changes in identity, relationships, independence, and decision-making.

Participants engage in mentorship, leadership exploration, educational enrichment, wellness activities, and experiences that encourage critical thinking, self-expression, healthy relationships, and personal responsibility.

Areas of focus: Identity, confidence, belonging, communication, decision-making, academic enrichment, leadership, and wellness.

Project Bridging — Grades 9–12 | Preparing for Leadership and Life Beyond Graduation

Our high school pathway emphasizes leadership, independence, advocacy, future planning, and real-world readiness.

Participants explore educational and career opportunities, financial literacy, entrepreneurship, civic participation, and leadership responsibilities while developing practical skills and building connections that support their next steps.

Areas of focus: Leadership, college and career readiness, financial capability, civic engagement, self-advocacy, professional development, and life skills.

Our Core Programs

Mentorship & Sisterhood

Relationships That Support Growth

Mentorship is at the heart of Project Bridging.

Through consistent engagement with trusted adult mentors, positive peer relationships, and meaningful group experiences, young women receive guidance and encouragement during important stages of their development.

Our approach recognizes the importance of being seen, heard, respected, and supported. Mentorship creates opportunities for honest conversations, relationship-building, shared learning, reflection, and personal growth.

Program experiences include:

  • Individual and group mentorship
  • Sisterhood circles and peer connection activities
  • Identity exploration and confidence-building
  • Goal-setting and personal reflection
  • Communication and conflict-resolution skills
  • Healthy relationships and positive decision-making
  • Support during developmental and educational transitions
  • Intergenerational conversations and community connections

What participants develop: Stronger relationships, self-awareness, confidence, communication skills, a sense of belonging, and supportive networks.

Bee University

Learning Beyond the Classroom

Bee University (BU) is Project Bridging’s educational enrichment and skill-development program.

Bee University connects academic learning, personal development, leadership, and practical life skills through engaging, culturally affirming, and experience-based activities.

Rather than approaching learning as a collection of unrelated subjects, Bee University encourages young women to understand how knowledge and skills connect to their everyday lives, interests, communities, and futures.

Bee University Learning Areas

Literacy & Critical Thinking

Reading, writing, discussion, research, media literacy, analysis, and understanding multiple perspectives.

STEM & Innovation

Science, technology, engineering, mathematics, creative problem-solving, design thinking, and hands-on exploration.

Leadership & Identity

Self-awareness, character development, personal responsibility, communication, decision-making, and leadership practice.

Financial Literacy

Budgeting, saving, spending, financial decision-making, banking fundamentals, and financial goal-setting.

Entrepreneurship & Innovation

Business ideas, product development, entrepreneurship, marketing, innovation, and collaborative problem-solving.

Civic Education & Social Justice

Civic participation, community issues, democratic processes, advocacy, equity, and informed community engagement.

College & Career Readiness

Career exploration, postsecondary pathways, workplace expectations, professional communication, and future planning.

Creative Arts & Expression

Visual arts, storytelling, digital media, performance, cultural expression, and creative communication.

Wellness & Healthy Living

Emotional awareness, self-care, healthy relationships, stress management, and personal well-being.

Life Skills & Personal Development

Time management, organization, etiquette, problem-solving, daily living skills, and responsible decision-making.

The Bee University Learning Journey

Learn → Belong → Serve → Lead

Participants gain knowledge, develop connections, apply their learning through service and real-world experiences, and practice leadership.

What participants develop: Stronger critical thinking, academic engagement, creative problem-solving, practical life skills, leadership abilities, and readiness for future opportunities.

Leadership Development & Youth Voice

From Participation to Leadership

At Project Bridging, leadership is something young women learn by practicing.

Our leadership programming creates opportunities for mentees to develop their voices, make decisions, organize activities, take responsibility, and contribute to matters affecting their lives and communities.

Through structured leadership roles and participatory experiences, young women learn that their perspectives are valuable and that they have a role in shaping the spaces and communities around them.

Leadership Opportunities

Youth Leadership Advisory Council

A youth-centered space where mentees contribute ideas, share feedback, identify concerns, and participate in program planning and decision-making.

Mentee Leadership Officers

Elected or appointed youth leaders have opportunities to practice communication, organization, teamwork, representation, and accountability through designated leadership responsibilities.

Public Speaking & Advocacy

Participants develop the skills to express ideas, present information, communicate concerns, and advocate for themselves and their communities.

Civic Leadership Experiences

Youth town halls, debates, mock trials, community conversations, and other civic learning activities introduce young women to leadership in public and community settings.

Service Leadership

Young women help plan and participate in service projects that respond to identified needs and strengthen community connections.

What participants develop: Leadership identity, self-advocacy, public speaking, teamwork, decision-making, civic awareness, and accountability.

Our Signature Initiatives

Focused Learning, Resources, and Experiences

Our signature initiatives provide focused learning, resources, and experiences that complement the broader Project Bridging program.

Thynk Pynk

Wellness. Identity. Confidence. Self-Care.

Thynk Pynk is Project Bridging’s wellness initiative focused on the physical, emotional, and social well-being of young women.

The initiative creates opportunities for age-appropriate conversations about identity, emotional wellness, self-image, healthy relationships, personal boundaries, confidence, and self-care.

Through workshops, guided discussions, creative activities, and intergenerational experiences, participants explore what wellness means in their own lives and how healthy choices can support their personal growth.

Program experiences may include:

  • Emotional wellness and stress-management workshops
  • Identity and self-image conversations
  • Healthy relationship education
  • Personal boundaries and self-advocacy
  • Self-care and wellness practices
  • Intergenerational discussions
  • Creative expression and reflection
  • Community wellness experiences

Signature: What’s Your Pynk? — A celebration of identity, individuality, confidence, resilience, and the many ways young women define themselves.

Project Pocketbook

Menstrual Equity & Education

Project Pocketbook addresses menstrual equity by increasing access to menstrual care products, education, and supportive resources.

We recognize that lack of access to menstrual products and accurate information can create barriers to comfort, dignity, school participation, and daily activities.

Through product distribution, education, partnerships, and awareness activities, Project Pocketbook works to reduce period-related barriers.

Initiative activities include:

  • Menstrual product collection and distribution
  • Menstrual health education
  • Personal care resource kits
  • Period poverty awareness
  • Community donation drives
  • Resource partnerships

Our focus: Dignity, education, access, and participation.

Supply the Hive

Hygiene, Essentials & Everyday Support

Supply the Hive helps connect young women with hygiene products, personal care supplies, and other essential items that support daily well-being.

Through community donations, sponsor contributions, and coordinated distribution, the initiative helps reduce practical barriers associated with limited access to essential supplies.

Examples of resources include:

  • Toothbrushes, toothpaste, and oral care products
  • Soap, deodorant, and body care essentials
  • Shampoo, conditioner, and hair care supplies
  • Personal hygiene products
  • Seasonal care packages
  • Other essential items identified through participant needs

Our focus: Meeting practical needs with care, dignity, and respect.

Bee Empowered

Financial Literacy & Financial Confidence

Bee Empowered introduces young women to financial knowledge and skills that support responsible decision-making and future economic independence.

Through interactive learning and real-world examples, participants explore money management, financial goal-setting, and the role financial decisions play in their lives.

Learning experiences include:

  • Budgeting and saving
  • Needs versus wants
  • Banking and financial services
  • Understanding credit and debt
  • Consumer awareness
  • Financial goal-setting
  • Introduction to investing and wealth-building concepts

What participants develop: Financial awareness, decision-making skills, and practical money-management knowledge.

Bee the Boss

Entrepreneurship. Innovation. Ownership.

Bee the Boss introduces young women to entrepreneurship as a pathway for creativity, problem-solving, leadership, and economic opportunity.

Participants explore what it takes to develop an idea, identify a customer need, create a product or service, and bring a business concept to life.

Learning experiences include:

  • Business idea development
  • Entrepreneurship fundamentals
  • Branding and marketing
  • Product creation
  • Pricing and budgeting
  • Customer service
  • Pitch presentations
  • Youth entrepreneurship showcases and markets

Signature: Bee the Boss Market — An opportunity for young women to present, promote, and sell products or share their business ideas while practicing entrepreneurship skills.

Bridging Brilliance

Etiquette. Confidence. Presentation. Personal Growth.

Bridging Brilliance introduces young women to social etiquette, personal presentation, communication, and the confidence to navigate different social and professional environments.

Through workshops, cultural experiences, formal event preparation, and practical learning, participants develop skills that support respectful interactions and greater comfort in unfamiliar settings.

Learning experiences include:

  • Social and dining etiquette
  • Professional communication
  • Personal presentation
  • Confidence and composure
  • Public speaking
  • Social awareness
  • Cultural experiences
  • Formal event preparation

Signature: Bridging Brilliance celebration and recognition.

Experiential Learning & Educational Excursions

The World Is Part of the Classroom

Experiential learning is a defining part of Project Bridging.

We believe young women benefit from seeing, exploring, and participating in experiences that introduce them to different environments, institutions, industries, cultures, and opportunities.

Our educational excursions connect classroom concepts and personal interests to the real world.

These experiences are intentionally connected to learning goals, reflection, skill development, and opportunities for continued exploration.

Educational Experience Areas

College & University Exposure

Campus visits, college fairs, HBCU exploration, conversations with students and professionals, and postsecondary readiness experiences.

STEM & Career Exploration

Science centers, innovation spaces, technology experiences, career-related site visits, and hands-on learning opportunities.

History, Culture & Civic Education

Museums, historical sites, cultural institutions, government spaces, and educational experiences that connect history to contemporary issues.

Leadership & Team-Building

Retreats, outdoor education, collaborative challenges, leadership workshops, and shared learning activities.

Arts & Cultural Experiences

Performances, exhibitions, museums, cultural celebrations, and experiences that expand artistic and cultural understanding.

Featured Educational Expedition

The Hive Takes Disney — STEM, Leadership & Global Learning. An educational expedition concept connecting STEM exploration, creative thinking, teamwork, career awareness, and global perspectives through an immersive learning experience.

Like other major excursions, participation depends on program scheduling, fundraising, partnerships, and confirmed arrangements.

Beyond the Experience

Educational excursions are supported by preparation and reflection activities so participants can connect what they encounter to what they are learning.

We encourage mentees to document their experiences, share their perspectives, and bring new knowledge back to their peers and communities.

Community Service, Civic Engagement & Advocacy

Learning to Lead Change

Community engagement provides young women with opportunities to understand the issues affecting their communities and explore how they can respond through informed action.

Project Bridging incorporates service, civic education, and youth advocacy into its developmental experiences.

Participants are encouraged to examine issues, ask meaningful questions, listen to different perspectives, and contribute to solutions.

Areas of Engagement

Community Service

Participating in volunteer projects, collection drives, neighborhood initiatives, and community improvement activities.

Civic Learning

Understanding local government, community decision-making, civic responsibilities, and democratic processes.

Youth Advocacy

Identifying issues affecting young people, developing informed perspectives, and learning how to communicate concerns and proposed solutions.

Youth Town Halls

Creating opportunities for young women to discuss issues, ask questions, and engage with community leaders.

Debate & Mock Trial

Building critical thinking, public speaking, research, reasoning, and communication skills through structured activities.

What participants develop: Civic knowledge, social responsibility, collaborative problem-solving, advocacy skills, and practical leadership experience.

College, Career & Life Readiness

Preparing for What’s Next

As young women move toward adulthood, Project Bridging provides opportunities to explore future pathways and develop skills that support independence and informed decision-making.

Our college, career, and life readiness activities connect education with practical preparation for the next stages of life.

College & Postsecondary Exploration

  • Exploring different colleges and educational pathways
  • HBCU exposure and awareness
  • Understanding admissions and financial aid basics
  • Scholarship awareness
  • Campus visits and college fairs
  • Postsecondary goal-setting

Career & Workforce Exploration

  • Career discovery and interest exploration
  • Workplace expectations and professionalism
  • Resume and interview preparation
  • Networking and professional communication
  • Career conversations with professionals
  • Introduction to internships, training, and employment pathways

Life Skills

  • Time management and organization
  • Personal responsibility
  • Communication and problem-solving
  • Conflict resolution
  • Goal-setting and decision-making
  • Independent living fundamentals
  • Digital responsibility and online safety

These experiences help mentees connect their current decisions and learning to future opportunities.

Family Engagement & Community Support

Stronger Connections. Greater Support.

Families play an important role in the Project Bridging experience.

We recognize that consistent communication, supportive relationships, and access to community resources can help young women remain engaged and participate meaningfully.

Project Bridging seeks to establish collaborative relationships with parents, caregivers, mentors, and community partners.

Family engagement activities include:

  • Parent and caregiver orientation
  • Family communication and program updates
  • Family participation opportunities
  • Resource sharing and referrals
  • Community gatherings
  • Conversations about participant needs and experiences
  • Support for consistent program engagement

When families need assistance beyond Project Bridging’s direct services, we seek to connect them with appropriate community resources and organizations.

The Project Bridging Experience

Learn. Connect. Explore. Serve. Lead.

Project Bridging is designed to provide ongoing engagement rather than isolated activities.

Participants have opportunities to connect multiple areas of learning and development throughout the program year.

Our planned program rhythm includes:

Weekly

Mentorship, educational enrichment, skill-building, personal reflection, and group engagement.

Monthly

Focused workshops, signature learning themes, community service, enrichment activities, and youth development experiences.

Quarterly

Leadership experiences, youth voice opportunities, special workshops, and community engagement activities.

Throughout the Year

Educational excursions, cultural experiences, college and career exploration, community partnerships, special projects, and celebrations.

Specific schedules and activity availability may vary by age group, program year, funding, and community partnerships.
